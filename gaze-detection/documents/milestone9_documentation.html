Milestone 9 · Cheat sheet & technical reference

Gaze Snake

File: milestone9_snake_game.py Input: eyes only — gaze steers, blink presses Writes data: no Version: 2026-07-21

Snake played entirely with the eyes. You steer by looking where you want the snake to go, and press the two on-screen buttons by blinking. It reuses the whole tracking stack from milestones 4 and 7 — the positioning gate, drift correction and calibrated gaze cursor — and adds nothing to the dataset.

Part 1

Cheat sheet — how to play

1.1 Before you start

The game needs a calibration file. If you have never calibrated, or you have moved your chair or laptop since, do this first:

$env:GAZE_USER = "arsi"              # tag your data (once per terminal)
python milestone4_calibration.py     # ~100 s — creates calibration_model.pkl
python milestone9_snake_game.py

Without a calibration the game refuses to start and tells you so.

1.2 The three things that happen at launch

1
Get into position
Sit 45–65 cm away, face centred. Hold GREEN for 2 seconds and it moves on.
2
Drift check — look at the centre dot
Measures how your posture differs from calibration day and subtracts it. Takes about 3 seconds.
3
GET READY — 2 seconds to aim
The snake does not move yet, but steering is already live. Look where you want it to launch.

1.3 Controls

To do thisDo thisNotes
Steer the snakeLook where you want it to go It turns toward your gaze. It will not reverse into itself, and it will not drive into a wall.
Eat foodLook at the orange dot Aim assist pulls you onto it once you are close. The zone is invisible by design.
Press RESTART / QUITLook at the button, then blink Wait for it to light up and say BLINK NOW. You then have 2 seconds.
Emergency exitq on the keyboard Always works, in case gaze control gets stuck.
Blinking is only for buttons. Blinking while playing does nothing — the cursor simply freezes for that instant and carries on. You cannot accidentally crash by blinking.

1.4 What is on the screen

Bright green squareThe snake's head — the end that moves.

1.5 Tips

  • Look ahead of the snake, not at it. Looking at the head itself is a dead zone and gives no command.
  • Look well away from the head to turn decisively. A glance just beside it produces a weak signal; a clear look up or down turns cleanly.
  • Do not chase the yellow cursor. Look at the target; let the cursor come to you. Chasing your own cursor creates a feedback loop.
  • Blink deliberately on buttons — a firm, normal blink. Fluttering may fall inside the 0.6 s cooldown and register only once.
  • If steering feels biased in one direction, that is drift: quit and relaunch so the drift check re-measures.

2.3 Steering algorithm

Called every frame; the snake only moves every tick, so several steers occur between moves. Candidate directions are ranked by how much of the gap to the aim point each closes, then filtered in three tiers:

1. best requested direction that is neither a reversal nor fatal
2. otherwise turn perpendicular, preferring the side you are looking toward
3. otherwise any legal escape; if none, keep heading (genuinely trapped)

A dead zone of 0.6 × CELL around the head produces no command, so looking at the snake itself holds the current course.

Safety test

def is_safe(self, direction):
    nx, ny = head + direction
    if out of bounds: return False
    # the tail vacates its cell this tick, so it only blocks when eating
    occupied = self.body if (nx, ny) == self.food else list(self.body)[1:]
    return (nx, ny) not in occupied
Why obey-blindly does not work here. Gaze is accurate to about 2 cm. Near an edge, a stray reading points into the wall; a keyboard player would never issue that command, but the tracker does it constantly. Filtering fatal directions is what makes the game playable rather than a slot machine. Verified: 30 games × 100 fully random aim points produce 0 deaths.

2.4 Blink detection

Uses the ear_left / ear_right features from gaze_features.py — eye aspect ratio, roughly 0.30 open and 0.10 shut.

ConstantValueRole
BLINK_EAR0.18Mean EAR below this counts as shut
BLINK_COOLDOWN0.6 sMinimum spacing between registered blinks

Edge-triggered on the open→shut transition, so holding the eyes closed fires once rather than every frame.

2.5 Button lock — and the problem it solves

The problem. Closing your eyes makes the gaze network read closed eyelids and emit nonsense angles. The cursor therefore leaps away at the exact moment you blink to press a button — the press always missed.

Two independent fixes:

  1. Cursor freeze. While EAR is below the blink threshold the cursor stops updating entirely and holds its last good position. Applies everywhere, not just to buttons.
  2. Dwell lock. After 0.25 s of gaze on a button it locks and ignores gaze completely for 2 seconds, pinning the cursor to its centre.
idle ──hover 0.25s──▶ LOCKED ──blink──▶ pressed
                        │
                        └──2.0s, no blink──▶ released ──0.8s refractory──▶ idle
ConstantValueRole
BUTTON_DWELL0.25 sGaze rest required before locking
LOCK_WINDOW2.0 sHow long the lock waits for a blink
LOCK_REFRACTORY0.8 sPause before the same button can re-lock

2.6 Aim assist

One shared magnet() function: full lock inside snap, linear fade out to radius, nothing beyond. Applied in two scopes that cannot overlap.

ScopeSnapRadiusActive wherePurpose
Buttons95 px · 2.5 cm260 px · 7 cm y ≥ 520 onlyStrong — must exceed the button's own 130 px half-width
Food55 px · 1.5 cm190 px · 5 cm Play area onlyWeaker — you must still be able to steer away deliberately
Sizing rule. The radius must exceed the target's own size plus the tracker's error, or the assist never engages. The first version used 110 px against a button whose half-width was 130 px — a gaze landing on the button's edge got no help at all, which is why it felt inert.

Measured pull toward food: 40 px away → snapped on; 100 px → pulled to 33 px; 150 px → pulled to 105 px; beyond 190 px → untouched.

2.7 Bug history

Three defects produced an immediate, unprovoked GAME OVER. All are fixed and covered by regression tests.

Bug 1 — reversal checked against the pending direction

moving right, body (14,7) (15,7) (16,7), head at (16,7)
frame 1: steer → UP    (legal, not the reverse of right)
frame 2: steer → LEFT  (legal, not the reverse of UP)     ← slips through
tick:    head (16,7) → (15,7) = its own neck → dead, score 0

Fix: record last_dir when a move actually executes, and judge reversals against that rather than the pending direction.

Bug 2 — steering obeyed fatal commands

With the snake on the top row, a glance above it set direction UP and it drove into the wall one tick later. Fix: the three-tier safety filter in §2.3.

Bug 3 — no time to aim

The drift check leaves you looking at screen centre, which sits below the snake's start position, so it dived immediately. After RESTART you were still looking at the button. Fix: the 2-second grace period, during which steering is live but the snake does not move.

2.9 Frame loop order

read frame → gate.evaluate → gaze model (if in zone) → extract features
  ├─ EAR → eyes_shut?  → if shut, DO NOT update the cursor
  ├─ blink edge detect
  ├─ aim_px   = button_assist(gaze)        # only near the button strip
  ├─ steer_px = food_assist(gaze)          # only inside the play area
  ├─ buttons.update(aim_px) → locked?
  ├─ blink + locked → press (RESTART / QUIT)
  ├─ steer(steer_px)  unless dead, out of zone, or on a button
  ├─ step() every TICK, once grace has elapsed and in zone
  └─ draw board, buttons, HUD, intent line, cursor
Ordering matters. Buttons are resolved before steering so a blink meant for a button cannot also swing the snake, and the cursor freeze happens before anything reads the cursor.
